An Instruction Challenge for JOUR108

Recognizing Mainstream Media Control Over Agenda Setting

Reading Time: About 10 minutes
Difficulty: Easy

In the policymaking process, policymakers utilize the mainstream news media outlets to set the agenda in legislative meetings. The issue with using news outlets as a starting point for policy formulation is that the news outlets are privately owned: Meaning that they have their own individual motivations. To achieve their goals they can choose to cover specific events, or to not cover those events. The tone in which the media is written and said is another crucial (often looked over by the audience) method of the news outlets of establishing a bias. This is essential to agenda setting because in a democracy, the public support for policy is essential because elected politicians are individuals as well. They want to appeal to their voters and not risk being voted out in a future election. This has a ripple effect to the way other elected politicians vote. Politicians, particularly senators, tend to vote based on cues from other party members when it comes to voting on issues they are not specialized and familiar in.

Because it is impossible for the average working class person to know the exact truth about what is happening in the world, and in the country, it is essential to recognize and degrade the institutional trust that is automatically given to news outlets. It is also crucial that people learn to recognize the news that they can trust and to recognize how to trace the bias to its root (since these outlets have multiple layers). The trend of decentralizing the responsibility of setting the agenda from the hands of billion dollar news networks into the more dispersed individual content creators on social media has called for another threat to the truth that must not be given power by the audience. As the author of this resource, I have my own bias and motivation as anyone else. However, this guide will go over the though process of recognizing bias based on research and real world examples to show how anyone can apply these skills to their lives.

  1. Policymaking Framework (a quick overview of political science findings)

    To help political scientists understand how policy becomes proposed when it will happen, there have been an abundant of theories to help explain policy formulation. Some of these theories include: punctuated equilibrium, multiple streams framework, elite theory... What is the key take away from these frameworks is that they all include a window of opportunity in which policy can be formulated. The window of opportunity can be caused by a crisis, a problem that has been demanding to be addressed, or (for the purposes of this resource) by the media. What this means is that the mainstream media, and what they choose to cover or not to cover, has a significant control over what is deemed a relevant problem to be addressed.   3. Public Opinion Matters, Which is Why the Media is a Valuable Agenda Setting Tool

    As of April 25, 2025, President Donald Trump's approval ratings have been falling at a dramatic rate. The only area of approval where he has been above water is his handling on imagration. However, because the mainstream media has been increasingly covering the Trump administration and the errors that they have made in defying the supreme court rulings and 14th amendment, his approval rating have begun to fall on immigration as well. His approval rating plummeting in the economy has resulted in him rolling back tariff policies against China, an example of the importance of public opinion being crucial in policymaking. These examples showcase how the media plays a role in policy formulation and the policy evaluation stage. Without the coverage that media companies provided of the controversial policy actions the administration has taken, there would be no attention given to the problem.

  4. Now Its Time to Understand How the Media Has its Own Interest in Policymaking

    Like any other company, the mainstream media news outlets are privately owned meaning that they have their own interests. One way in which news outlets operate is the consistent need for story beats. One easy beat to consistently have on the news is global affairs and what occurs in Washington. Media outlets can send journalists to military bases and the capital to get frequent stories. The problem that arises is that because the military and politicians are the source of the information being fed to the public, the starting point of information is inherently biased. Political science research demonstrates that the media labels victims as worthy or unwhorthy of receiving attention based on the ties that states around the world have with the US government. For example, statitically Israeli soldiers kill more palestinians than vice versa, and there are significantly more displaced palestinians than Israeli, since it was Israel that invaded Palestine under the claim to target only Hamas. However, media coverage showcases more Israel refugees and prisoners released than palestinians. This weaponizes the empathy and emotional response of the consumer of the media into believe that the conflict was an equal dispute rather than a genocide as mentioned by the United Nations Court of Justice.

    This is an excerpt from the Pew Research Center: https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2020/08/31/ americans-who-have-a-closer-relationship-with-their-news-sources-are-more-supportive-of-the-news-media-overall/

    Other ways media will be biased is the appeal to profit. Because news outlets need to generate profit, they need to appeal to advertisers or donnors in order to maintain revenue. This adds another dimension of bias that must be considered when consuming media. It is also possible for the owners of the networks to have investments into different industries, so the need to not cover certain issues depends on the owner. For example, in the 2024 presidential election cycle, Jeff Bezos, who ownes the Washington Post, refused to allow the news outlet to cover certain parts of the Harris Wallz Campaign.

  5. How to Apply This Knowledge Into Your Personal Life and Become More Independent

    What's the point of understanding bias and policymaking in media? The answer is that it makes people more independent and informed. This resource is only a brief overview of the complex nature of how mainstream news outlets operate. The examples mentioned in previous steps depict relevant ways in which to apply political theory and research findings. You might begin to notice how this resource itself is biased. I chose to cover an example of how the Trump presidency chooses falls to public opinion, which is one more example than I gave any democratic presidency. The frequency at which media is covered is essential to public opinion. Just because I chose to not use a previous democratic presidency, because Trump is a relevant and current example, does not mean that they are exempt from the same ways in which media influences them and their policies. The lesson: broaden view points and zoom out to see a bigger picture, this often includes looking at quantified data sets or a source that would be less biased. Even if you do not consume the mainstream media news outlets, social media is not excluded from the basic principles of agenda-setting.
